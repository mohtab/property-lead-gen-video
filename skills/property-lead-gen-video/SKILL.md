# Property Lead Gen Video

Create three production-ready creative packages using the same eight-part structure. Include an outline, exact timed script, storyboard and paste-ready AI video prompt pack for each variation, plus voiceover when requested or useful. Keep the message steady while the visuals change. Build a specific buyer argument, with imagery that demonstrates each claim. Distinguish a ready-to-use production script from a rendered video.

## 1. Read the brief and extract facts

Read supplied materials before asking questions. Accept brochures, sales decks, approved copy, footage folders, renders, floor plans and rough notes. Use available file-reading tools; require no particular connector or private workspace.

Extract these inputs:

| Input | Needed information |
|---|---|
| Property facts | Approved project name, location, property types, sizes, features, amenities, development and delivery status |
| Buyer | Priority buyer or tenant, motivation and main hesitation |
| Objective | One desired action: enquiry, appointment, viewing, registration or reservation |
| Commercial facts | Publishable price and currency, price basis, terms, availability, validity date and source |
| Visuals | Actual filenames, content, usable source in/out points when inspected, orientation and quality |
| Distribution | Language, channel, placement, aspect ratio and requested duration |
| Brand and action | Logos, tone, required wording, disclosures, CTA destination and functional next step |

Keep a compact fact ledger: claim, source locator, approval/publication status and date where relevant. Do not treat a brochure as proof that a price is still current or approved for advertising. Keep unresolved items visibly separate from approved copy.

Default to three 25-second treatments, vertical 9:16, text-led and understandable without sound. Propose 1080 × 1920 as a working master size. Use the user's language unless a campaign language is specified. Default to an editable treatment in chat; create documents only when requested or clearly useful.

Ask only for missing information that materially changes the work, preferably one short batch covering the buyer, action and missing factual materials. Do not send a blank questionnaire when supplied documents already contain the answers. If answers are unavailable, produce useful provisional treatments with labeled assumptions and placeholders; never invent property details.

## 2. Preserve the eight-part structure

Use the following timings for the standard 25-second version. Preserve all eight purposes and their order across all three variations. These are editorial defaults, not experimentally proven conversion benchmarks.

| Timeline | Duration | Purpose | Content |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 0–3 s | 3 s | Distinction | Property name and strongest specific promise |
| 3–5.5 s | 2.5 s | Place | District, city or relevant location context |
| 5.5–9 s | 3.5 s | Product | One tangible benefit demonstrated visually |
| 9–12 s | 3 s | Lifestyle | A human moment showing the experience |
| 12–15 s | 3 s | Choice | Property types or another meaningful differentiator |
| 15–18.5 s | 3.5 s | Offer | Approved starting price or release proposition |
| 18.5–20.5 s | 2 s | Availability | A factual reason to act |
| 20.5–25 s | 4.5 s | Action | Branding, one clear CTA and its destination |

Identify the property immediately. Treat its name as a consistent identity element when appropriate; include its reading burden when checking the opening.

If price is unavailable, use a sourced launch or release proposition, or a verified service such as a viewing invitation. If inventory or scarcity is unconfirmed, use a supported availability statement such as a confirmed viewing service. Otherwise leave a marked placeholder for approval. Never manufacture scarcity, a launch, a service or a deadline to fill a slot.

Use confirmed information access as a fallback when the user approves a brochure-request service: make Offer the specific information proposition (for example, exploring the known apartment options) and Availability the confirmed access status (for example, “Brochure on request”). Do not imply available inventory. Keep the final Action as the actual brochure CTA. Distinguish a missing destination URL from an unconfirmed service; a pending URL alone does not invalidate an explicitly approved brochure-request action. If no commercial or information proposition is supported, retain placeholders.

Only change duration or sequence when the user asks or a concrete constraint requires it; explain any departure. Recalculate every interval. For leasing, adapt product, choice and commercial language to space, terms and viewings while retaining the narrative purposes. For non-residential properties, interpret lifestyle as the human experience of using the space.

## 3. Develop three distinct variations

Use these default buyer motivations:

1. **Life at the property** — Lead with a supported lived benefit. Emphasize comfort, daily routines, belonging or the experience of the space.
2. **What makes it distinctive** — Lead with a demonstrable difference. Emphasize architecture, layouts, materials, amenities or services supported by the brief.
3. **The opportunity to act** — Lead toward a practical decision. Emphasize product fit, verified commercial terms, current availability and the next step.

Change the hook, supporting argument and visual priorities meaningfully. Do not provide three paraphrases. Keep the same buyer, primary action, material facts, duration and brand system unless the user requests different audiences or funnel stages. Carry the different emphasis through the fixed eight beats; do not move price to the opening simply because a variation is commercially focused.

Give each variation a one-sentence premise, the buyer motivation it addresses and the reason it differs. Treat multi-element creative variants as concept comparisons. Only describe a test as isolating a single cause when only that variable changes.

## 4. Write copy and build the edit

Use [references/treatment-format.md](references/treatment-format.md) for the output schema, editorial timing checks and adaptations. Load it when producing treatments.

Write concrete, restrained copy. Replace generic adjectives with sourced benefits. Use one primary message at a time, generally 3–7 words and no more than two lines. Give longer text more reading time or shorten it to fit the fixed slot. Avoid dense lists, unsupported superlatives, investment return promises and invented proximity claims.

Separate the message timeline from the shot timeline. Hold one message across two or more shots where useful. Do not restart its entrance animation at every cut. Aim for 12–15 moving shots plus the closing card; use mostly 1–2 second shots, with occasional longer hero views or brief inserts. Treat the shot count as a target, not a reason to invent footage.

Map shots to inspected assets by exact filename. Mark uninspected filenames as unverified. Distinguish actual footage, renders, stock lifestyle material and suggested new captures. Never imply a render shows a completed property or stock footage depicts its location or facilities. Preserve required render and development-stage disclosures. Avoid generating invented architecture or amenities as factual product evidence.

Keep one CTA tied to a real destination. Do not promise instant booking, available viewing slots or reservation functionality unless supplied. Mark missing routes as unresolved.

## 5. Prepare the production package

Deliver all of the following for each variation, following the reference schema:
1. A short creative outline explaining the hook, progression and action.
2. A timed script with exact on-screen wording, display spans and readable holds.
3. A shot-by-shot storyboard mapped to the script and supplied assets. A concrete text storyboard is sufficient by default; generate storyboard images only when requested and supported.
4. A paste-ready AI video prompt pack: one master creative brief, self-contained per-shot prompts and assembly instructions. Make it usable in tools such as Higgsfield without relying on unverified product features or a particular integration.
5. Optional voiceover: exact speakable narration, timed delivery and a separate clean recording/TTS copy when requested or when narration materially helps. Otherwise state that the concept is text-led and VO is unnecessary.
6. Source/asset mapping, missing inputs, CTA route and concise editor notes.

For the prompt pack and VO, load [references/ai-video-and-voiceover.md](references/ai-video-and-voiceover.md). Provide the actual prompts and script, not instructions telling the user to write them. Carry the three different creative angles through the prompts. Keep unresolved facts clearly marked; do not label a package ready to publish while approval placeholders remain. If the user wants only concepts, shorten presentation without dropping the eight-part logic.

## 6. Check and deliver

Before handoff:
- Sum message slots and shot durations independently to 25 seconds; eliminate gaps and unintended overlaps.
- Distinguish animation-inclusive display span from fully readable hold.
- Check every line at phone size, including project names, prices, qualifiers and disclosures.
- Shorten a 9-word or longer primary message in a slot under 4 seconds, or explicitly retime the film with the user's agreement. Count all concurrently displayed headline words, not only one line.
- Verify each claim, date, currency, price basis and availability statement against supplied evidence.
- Match visual proof to the message and flag asset gaps explicitly.
- Check logos, text and disclosures against current placement overlays when available; otherwise flag placement QA as pending, not completed.
- Keep Arabic and English in separate versions by default; review translation, reading direction, line breaks and timing independently. If bilingual is requested, reduce copy or propose a revised duration.
- Make no claim that a finished video was rendered, checked, published or performance-tested when only a treatment was created.

For measurement, prioritize qualified enquiries, booked viewings or appointments according to the objective, alongside retention and completion. Recommend tests as hypotheses; do not promise conversion results.

## Public portability

Keep this skill brand-neutral and self-contained. Do not bundle customer documents, private project facts, uploaded references, credentials, local workspace paths or internal operating models into the shared skill. Use a campaign's supplied facts only in that campaign's outputs. Do not depend on personal memories or installed proprietary tools. Saving a skill does not publish a campaign or authorize spending, uploads to advertising accounts, or outreach.
